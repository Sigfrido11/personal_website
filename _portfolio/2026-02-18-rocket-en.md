# Building a C++20 Orbital Rocket Simulator: Physics, Propulsion, and Architecture

Simulating a launch vehicle from the launchpad to orbit requires coupling aerodynamics, thermodynamics, and orbital mechanics within a numerically stable integration framework.  
This simulator, developed in **C++20** with **SFML**, models a physically consistent ascent from liftoff to orbital insertion, avoiding oversimplified kinematic approximations.

Below is a detailed breakdown of the physical models, numerical strategies, and architectural design choices behind the project.

---

## 1. The Atmosphere Is a Stratified Thermodynamic System

A simple exponential density decay is insufficient for realistic ascent simulations. Drag and propulsion performance depend critically on accurate thermodynamic state variables.

### International Standard Atmosphere (ISA)

The simulator implements the **ISA model**, computing atmospheric properties layer-by-layer:

- Troposphere  
- Tropopause  
- Stratosphere (up to ~51 km and extendable)

Each layer is modeled using:

Hydrostatic equilibrium:

$$
\frac{dP}{dh} = -\rho g
$$

Ideal gas law:

$$
P = \rho R T
$$

From these, the simulation derives altitude-dependent:

- Pressure $$P(h)$$  
- Density $$\rho(h)$$  
- Temperature $$T(h)$$  

### Impact on Propulsion

Rocket thrust depends on ambient pressure $$P_a$$:

$$
F = \dot{m} v_e + (P_e - P_a) A_e
$$

A realistic $$P_a(h)$$ enables accurate modeling of:

- Underexpanded flow near sea level  
- Optimal expansion at design altitude  
- Overexpansion and performance loss  

This coupling between thermodynamics and propulsion is essential for credible ascent dynamics.

---

## 2. Mach-Dependent Aerodynamics and the Transonic Regime

Drag is modeled as:

$$
F_d = \frac{1}{2} \rho v^2 C_d(M) A
$$

The critical complexity lies in the Mach-dependent drag coefficient $$C_d(M)$$.

### Drag Divergence

As the vehicle approaches Mach 1:

- Shock formation begins  
- Wave drag rises sharply  
- $$C_d$$ becomes highly nonlinear  

Piecewise definitions introduce discontinuities that destabilize numerical integration.

### Continuous Regime Blending

To avoid non-physical acceleration spikes, I implemented:

```cpp
double Cd_from_Mach(double M);
```

The function uses smooth hyperbolic tangent transitions:

$$
C_d(M) =
C_{sub}
+ \frac{C_{trans} - C_{sub}}{2} \left(1 + \tanh(k_1(M - M_1))\right)
+ \frac{C_{sup} - C_{trans}}{2} \left(1 + \tanh(k_2(M - M_2))\right)
+ \dots
$$

This guarantees:

- $C^1$ continuity  
- Stable acceleration profiles  
- Realistic transonic behavior  

The following regimes are blended continuously:

- Subsonic  
- Transonic  
- Supersonic  
- Hypersonic  

---

## 3. Modular Propulsion Architecture

A key design goal was decoupling propulsion physics from vehicle dynamics.

### Abstract Engine Interface

```cpp
class Engine {
public:
    virtual double delta_m(double dt) = 0;
    virtual sf::Vector2d thrust_vector(...) = 0;
    virtual ~Engine() = default;
};
```

Each engine implementation must compute:

- Mass flow rate  
- Thrust vector  

The rocket class remains agnostic to internal propulsion mechanics.

---

### Implemented Engine Models

#### 1. Base Engine (Isp Model)

A simplified thrust model:

$$
F = \dot{m} I_{sp} g_0
$$

Ideal for rapid trajectory prototyping.

---

#### 2. Advanced Solid Motor

Internal ballistics are modeled via regression rate:

$$
r = a P_c^n
$$

Chamber pressure evolution is coupled to burn surface area.

Exhaust velocity derived from isentropic flow:

$$
v_e =
\sqrt{
\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma - 1}
R T_c
\left(
1 -
\left(\frac{P_e}{P_c}\right)^{\frac{\gamma - 1}{\gamma}}
\right)
}
$$

This captures:

- Pressure-dependent burn rate  
- Grain geometry evolution  
- Time-varying thrust curves  

---

#### 3. Advanced Liquid Engine

Pump-fed engine model using characteristic velocity:

$$
\dot{m} = \frac{P_c A_t}{c^*}
$$

Where:

- $$A_t$$ = throat area  
- $$c^*$$ = characteristic velocity  

This enables chamber-pressure-based thrust computation with altitude coupling.

---

### Architectural Advantage

The rocket object simply queries:

```cpp
engine->thrust_vector(...)
```

This allows:

- Mixed propulsion stacks  
- Solid boosters + liquid core  
- Seamless runtime staging  

No modification of the physics loop is required when adding new propulsion types.

## 4. Orbital Mechanics and Numerical Stability

The simulation operates in polar coordinates $$(r, \theta)$$.

### Why Polar Coordinates?

Central gravity is naturally expressed as:

$$
F_g = -\frac{GMm}{r^2}
$$

Advantages:

- Exact curvature representation  
- No artificial Earth flattening  
- Direct handling of orbital velocity  

---

### Semi-Implicit Euler Integration

Standard explicit Euler causes energy drift at orbital velocities.

Instead, a **semi-implicit (symplectic) Euler scheme** is used:

1. Compute forces (thrust, gravity, drag, inertial terms)  
2. Update velocity:

$$
v_{n+1} = v_n + a_n \Delta t
$$

3. Update position:

$$
x_{n+1} = x_n + v_{n+1} \Delta t
$$

This ordering:

- Improves energy behavior  
- Reduces orbital drift  
- Enhances long-term numerical stability  

---

## 5. Robust Multi-Stage Handling

Multi-stage rockets introduce instantaneous mass discontinuities.

### Mass Accounting

Each stage tracks:

- Structural mass  
- Propellant mass  
- Engine instance  

Total mass computed via `std::accumulate`.

---

### Burnout Detection

When:

$$
m_{propellant} \le \dot{m} \Delta t
$$

The simulator:

- Detaches the stage  
- Removes structural mass  
- Switches engine pointer  

---

### Data-Driven Configuration

Rocket parameters are loaded at runtime from JSON:

- Stage count  
- Fuel masses  
- Engine types  
- Structural parameters  

This enables rapid iteration without recompilation.

---

## 6. Why C++20?

The codebase leverages modern language features:

- `std::filesystem` for cross-platform resource management  
- STL algorithms for efficient aggregation  
- Strong const-correctness  
- Explicit exception handling (`std::runtime_error`)  

Physical impossibilities (negative mass, zero radius, invalid states) trigger immediate failure rather than silent corruption.

---

# Conclusion

What began as a basic ascent simulator evolved into a structured implementation of:

- Stratified atmospheric thermodynamics  
- Compressible-flow aerodynamics  
- Internal propulsion modeling  
- Orbital mechanics  
- Structure-preserving numerical integration  

By rigorously separating propulsion, aerodynamics, and kinematics through polymorphic design, the simulator achieves both:

- Physical realism  
- Architectural scalability  

It provides a technically faithful numerical representation of the engineering and physical challenges involved in reaching orbit.
